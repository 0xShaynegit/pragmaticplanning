What Is a Resource Consent?

A resource consent is permission from the Queenstown Lakes District Council (QLDC) to carry out an activity that the District Plan does not allow as of right. Under the Resource Management Act 1991, any activity that contravenes a rule in the District Plan requires consent before work can begin.

Most property owners encounter resource consent when they want to build in a sensitive location, exceed height or setback limits, undertake earthworks, change the use of a building, or subdivide land. Getting the application right from the outset is the difference between a smooth 20-working-day non-notified process and a drawn-out, costly notification that stalls your project for months.

Types of Consent We Handle

Land Use

Land Use Consents

The most common type. Required when you want to use land or build in a way that breaches a District Plan rule. This includes residential alterations and additions that exceed height or coverage limits, commercial development, new dwellings in sensitive zones, and earthworks above the permitted threshold.

Subdivision

Subdivision Consents

Required any time you divide a title into two or more lots. QLDC subdivision rules vary significantly by zone. We assess your site, advise on the most viable subdivision structure, and coordinate with surveyors, engineers, and Council to get you to a Section 224(c) certificate.

Coastal / Lakefront

Coastal and Lakefront Consents

Properties within the Coastal Marine Area or adjoining the Queenstown Lakes district's network of significant water bodies require resource consent for most structures and land disturbance. These applications involve QLDC, Environment Southland, and often Aukaha on behalf of Kati Huirapa ki Puketeraki and Te Runanga o Moeraki.

Visitor Accommodation

Visitor Accommodation Consents

Short-term letting of residential properties requires resource consent in most QLDC residential zones. We specialise in this area. See our Airbnb and Short-Term Accommodation service for the full picture.

How the Process Works

Every resource consent application in QLDC follows the same statutory pathway under the RMA. Understanding each step is the first thing we explain to every new client, because surprises at the wrong stage kill projects.

  1. Pre-application meeting: We meet with you to assess the site, understand the proposal, and confirm what consent is required. We often request a pre-application meeting with QLDC at this stage to gauge how the processing planner will approach the application.
  2. Application preparation: We prepare the Assessment of Environmental Effects (AEE), coordinate any specialist reports (traffic, acoustics, landscape, geotechnical), and assemble the complete application package for lodgement.
  3. Lodgement and acceptance: QLDC has 10 working days to accept or reject the application. We stay across this closely and respond immediately if Council requests changes before acceptance.
  4. Processing: QLDC processes non-notified applications within 20 working days. During this period they may issue a Request for Further Information (RFI) under Section 92 of the RMA. We respond on your behalf.
  5. Decision: Council issues a written decision granting or declining consent, with conditions. We review the decision and advise on compliance requirements or, if warranted, appeal options.

Notification: What It Means and How We Avoid It

Notification is when QLDC decides your application must be publicly advertised, allowing neighbours and the wider public to submit in support or opposition. Limited notification targets affected parties directly. Full public notification is rare but significant, triggering a formal hearing process.

Notification adds months and cost. Our job is to prepare applications that meet the non-notified threshold by addressing the effects of a proposal head-on. We do not pad applications with boilerplate. We identify the actual issues, obtain specialist evidence where it matters, and write AEEs that give the processing planner what they need to make a confident decision without notification.

The Queenstown Lakes Context

QLDC is one of the most active consent authorities in New Zealand relative to its land area. The district's rapid growth, sensitive natural environment, and complex layering of national and local planning rules means resource consenting here is harder than most places in the country.

The Operative District Plan is supplemented by the Proposed District Plan (Stage 3 decisions were released in 2023), National Policy Statements including the NPS-UD and NPS-FM, and significant Ngai Tahu values that must be considered in all decisions. Getting an application through QLDC requires someone who knows the district plan inside out and has an established working relationship with the processing team.
