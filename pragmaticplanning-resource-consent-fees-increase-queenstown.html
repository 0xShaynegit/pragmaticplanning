Council Proposed Increase of Resource Consent Fees: Have Your Say

QLDC consulted on proposed increases to resource consent processing, building consent, and resource management engineering fees in 2016. Here is what was proposed, our analysis, and the top tips for keeping your consenting costs as low as possible.

[TL;DR: 2016 Fee Review]
  • Fees proposed to increase 5-25% (first review since 2009)
  • Planner rate: $134.90 to $145/hr, new Senior rate $165/hr
  • RM Engineering: $137.54 to $165/hr
  • Proposed fees in line with Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch
  • Best way to save: submit a complete, well-written application
  • Submission deadline was 31 May 2016 (closed)

Archived: This article covers the 2016 fee review consultation. Submission deadline was 5pm Tuesday 31 May 2016. Fees have been updated since. For current fee information, contact QLDC or check their website directly.

QLDC was consulting on its review of standardised fees for Planning and Development Services, including fees for Resource Consent processing, Building Consent processing, and Resource Management Engineering. These fees had not been reviewed since 2009 and affected anyone dealing with the Council, whether property developers or homeowners wanting to build an additional bedroom.

What Was Proposed

Fees and hourly charge-out rates were proposed to increase by an average of between 5% and 25%. The following changes were proposed for day-to-day matters:

[Proposed Fee Changes: 2016]

Resource Consent Processing

The fees charged by Council Planners to process resource consent applications were proposed to increase from $134.90 to $145.00 per hour (inclusive of GST). A new Senior Planner rate of $165.00 per hour was also introduced.

Resource Management Engineering

Fees for Resource Management Engineers providing technical input to resource consent processing were proposed to increase from $137.54 to $165.00 per hour.

Resource Consent Monitoring and Compliance

Monitoring and Compliance Officers are tasked with monitoring the conditions of granted resource consents and enforcing non-compliance with the District Plan. Fees were proposed to increase from $134.90 to $145.00 per hour.

Administrative Support

Administrative support fees were proposed to increase from $74.75 to $90.00 per hour. The fixed administration fee was proposed to increase from $180 to $225.

Pre-Application Meeting Fees

The first hour of one Council staff member's time remained free. Additional hours were billed at standard hourly rates. Complex proposals requiring multiple staff attracted an initial fee of $1,500.00.

How Resource Consent Charging Works

Upon lodging an application, the applicant pays an initial deposit to Council. At the time, this ranged from $512.50 to $1,025 for most straightforward applications. During processing, time is charged against this initial deposit. Upon completion, either a refund is due (extremely rarely) or further invoices are issued.

QLDC proposed a general increase in these initial deposit fees. In our opinion, the increase in deposit fees would not result in additional cost for obtaining resource consents. Currently the final cost of the vast majority of applications exceeds the initial deposit. The proposed fees are more representative of the actual cost of processing an application.

Our Analysis

The fees had not been reviewed since 2009, so the review was not unexpected. The proposed fees were compared to the main centres:

  • Auckland: $153 per hour (Council Planner, inclusive of GST)
  • Wellington: $155 per hour
  • Christchurch: $150 to $200 per hour

The proposed QLDC fee increase ($145 to $165 per hour) brought fees broadly in line with other councils and was not considered unreasonable in itself. However, raising processing fees further increases the cost of subdividing land, building, starting a business, and much more.

Top Tips to Reduce Council Consenting Fees

  1. Submit a well-written and complete application. This is the single most important way to reduce your Council fees. Unforeseen issues, poorly drafted applications, and inexperience with the consenting process all result in greater Council time spent, and therefore greater cost. Engaging a consultant is often more cost-effective than the hidden cost of a poorly prepared application.
  2. Use pre-application meetings for complex proposals. While there is an up-front charge for meetings longer than one hour, the service generally reduces the total cost of consent by identifying issues early and giving both parties a shared understanding of the proposal.
  3. Have your proposal fully worked out in detail before lodging. Once a resource consent is issued, there is usually a significant cost to make any changes via a consent variation.
  4. Keep on top of your consent conditions. Non-compliance with consent conditions will result in Monitoring and Enforcement Officers spending time to address the situation. You will end up paying for that time.
