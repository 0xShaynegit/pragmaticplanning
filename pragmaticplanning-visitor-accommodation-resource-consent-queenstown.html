Visitor Accommodation Resource Consent: The Rules for Queenstown Lakes District

A plain-language breakdown of when you need resource consent for visitor accommodation in Queenstown Lakes District. Covers friends and family use, homestay (bed and breakfast), holiday home registration, and full resource consent requirements.

[TL;DR]
  • Friends/family, no payment: permitted activity, no action required
  • Homestay up to 5 guests: register with QLDC, no resource consent needed
  • Holiday home up to 30 days/year (single let): register only
  • Multiple lets up to 90 days/year (3+ nights each): register only
  • Unlimited letting: resource consent required
  • Advertising on Airbnb/Bookabach counts toward your letting allowance even if unoccupied

Archived Content (2019 Update): The information on this page reflects the rules in place prior to the 2023 Environment Court decision. It is retained for reference purposes only. For the current rules, please see our updated page: Council's New Short-Term Visitor Accommodation Rules.

Visitor Accommodation Under Siege

The Queenstown Lakes District Council launched a major campaign targeting the illegal visitor accommodation use of houses within the District, whether as a holiday house let by the property owner or tenants sub-letting rooms. As reported in the New Zealand Herald, The Otago Daily Times, and on the Council's own website, an audit was undertaken by the Council.

As a result of the audit, 792 property owners were contacted and reminded that they needed to register their properties, apply for resource consent, or alternatively stop advertising their properties for short-term rent. This applied also where tenants had been sub-letting rooms in a house without the landlord's knowledge.

Under the Resource Management Act, land owners could face a fine of up to $300,000 and two years' imprisonment for non-compliance.

So, What Are the Rules?

It's rather complicated, but here is a plain-language breakdown:

[Use By Friends & Family]

No Payment Involved

Do you want to allow your home to be used by friends or family without any payment? No further action is required. This is a permitted activity and you do not need to contact the Council.

[Home Stay / Bed & Breakfast]

Living On-Site With Paying Guests

Do you want to use your home as a homestay (bed and breakfast) for a maximum of five guests, with you living on site at the same time? You need to register your homestay with the Council, but should not need a resource consent.

If you will have more than five guests, you will require a resource consent.

[Commercial Letting: Airbnb, Bookabach, NZ Holiday Homes]

Renting Out Your Property

Option 1 (1-2 nights/year, single let): Do you only want to rent your home or room for one single let of one or two nights per annum, such as over the New Year period? No further action is required. This is a permitted activity and you do not need to contact the Council.

Option 2 (up to 30 days/year, single let): Do you want to rent out for one single let per annum, for up to 30 days? For example, to one family over the entire Christmas and New Year period. You need to register your holiday home with the Council, but should not need a resource consent.

Option 3 (up to 90 days/year, multiple lets): Do you want to rent out on multiple occasions per calendar year, up to a maximum of 90 days in total, with each single let being three or more nights? You need to register your holiday home with the Council, but should not need a resource consent.

Option 4 (unlimited): Do you want the ultimate freedom: the ability to rent out as many times as you want, to any number of guests, for any length of time? You require a resource consent.

Important Note: Advertising your property on websites such as Airbnb or Bookabach is counted toward your letting allowances. If you are advertising your holiday home on Airbnb year-round, you still require a resource consent even if you only intend to actually rent for 90 days or fewer.

Will My Rates Go Up?

In most, but not all, cases: yes. More information can be found in the Council's own Guide to Visitor Accommodation. If you pursue a resource consent, you will likely be subject to the payment of a development contribution to the Council.

I Need a Resource Consent

With holiday homes in the District commanding strong rental values, it is no surprise that many home owners choose to pursue a resource consent for visitor accommodation for the ultimate flexibility. But it is not always a simple process.

The ease of obtaining resource consent for visitor accommodation, whether in Queenstown, Wanaka, Arrowtown, or elsewhere in the District, differs widely. It depends on many factors including the location of the property, type of property (apartment versus farmhouse), noise, neighbours, car parking, and more. In our experience, the biggest single factor is your neighbours, which is exactly why we continue to recommend good-neighbourly communication over the backyard fence.

Because of this, we suggest you get in touch and have a chat at no charge. We will not sugar-coat the truth: if we think there is little chance your application will be approved, we will let you know.

Do I Need Professional Holiday Home Management?

As part of the resource consent application, we need to supply details on how the property will be carefully managed when used for short-term accommodation, so as to not affect neighbours or the wider neighbourhood. The majority of clients we work with choose to have their holiday home managed by a professional property management agency, and we incorporate this into the application.

Whilst not a requirement, a property manager will assist you in meeting the conditions of your resource consent, in addition to promoting your property.
