How to Find Out What Your Neighbours Are Planning in Queenstown

QLDC makes building consent and resource consent information publicly accessible. Here is a five-step guide to using the QLDC Mapping Viewer and Edocs portal to find out exactly what your neighbours have applied to build.

[TL;DR: 5 Steps]
  • Step 1: Ask your neighbours directly
  • Step 2: Find Assessment Number via QLDC Mapping Viewer
  • Step 3: Register and log in to QLDC Edocs
  • Step 4: Search by Assessment Number
  • Step 5: View Resource Consents or Building Consents tab
  • If not on Edocs: contact QLDC directly

We know the story. We have seen it many times. One day you are sitting in your living room enjoying that view. The next day a large shed has magically appeared on your neighbour's section. This surprise could have been avoided had your neighbours done the neighbourly thing and consulted with you, even if they were not legally required to.

Unfortunately that does not always happen. However, there is a way to find out exactly what those neighbours are up to.

Step 1: Ask Them

Stick your head over the fence and ask them. You may be surprised just how drama-free this really is.

Step 2: Find Your Neighbour's Assessment Number

Failing a direct conversation, the Queenstown Lakes District Council holds publicly accessible property information online that anyone can access to find out what has been applied for on a neighbouring property. The first step is to find the Assessment Number of your neighbour's property.

Go to the QLDC Mapping Viewer and search the address of the property in the search box. The property will appear along with a snapshot of the relevant property information. The Assessment Number appears in the left portion of the screen. Take note of this number as you will need it for the next step.

Step 3: Log In to QLDC Edocs

Head over to the Council's Edocs website. Edocs is a publicly accessible portal where you can view property information, including building and resource consents. If this is your first time using Edocs, you must first register (this takes approximately one minute). Then log in.

Step 4: Search by Assessment Number

In the search box, type or paste the Assessment Number for the property you found on the QLDC Mapping Viewer. This will produce a result below the search box. Click on this number. Edocs will then display all property information for the property you selected.

Step 5: View the Consent Applications

Select either the Resource Consents tab (for Planning Permission applications) or Building Consents (for Building Permits). This will display all building and resource consents currently linked to the property, along with a description of what each consent relates to.

Click on the (+) symbol to see all electronic and scanned documents associated with that consent. In most cases, you will want to view the plans to fully understand what is proposed. Click the download button to view a PDF of the document.

There you go: snooping on the neighbours made easy. If you know of a consent application that does not show up on Edocs, it may be best to contact the Council directly and make an enquiry.
