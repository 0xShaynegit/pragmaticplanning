Council's New Short-Term Visitor Accommodation Rules for Queenstown Lakes District

Zone-by-zone breakdown of the new Airbnb and short-term rental rules following the 2023 Environment Court decision. Covers the 90-night rule, registration process, and when a resource consent is legally required.

[TL;DR]
  • Up to 90 nights/year in most residential zones = registration only, no full resource consent
  • Up to 42 nights/year in Jacks Point (Hanley's Farm, Willowpond)
  • Over 90 nights = resource consent required, neighbours often need to approve
  • Homestay (you live on-site): up to 5 guests, year-round, no consent needed
  • All approved activities trigger a ~25% rates increase from QLDC
  • Unit title complexes: the 90-night allowance applies to the whole complex, not each unit

Explained: The New Short-Term Visitor Accommodation (Airbnb) Rules in Queenstown Lakes District

Information current as of January 2026.

On 30 January 2023, New Zealand's Environment Court issued a decision that has directed the Queenstown Lakes District Council to change the District Plan rules regarding short-term accommodation. This Court decision is relevant to anyone who intends to undertake short-term accommodation from their property in the District.

This page will provide a simple explanation of these new rules for the three main District Plan zones in Queenstown Lakes District (including Wanaka and Hawea), plus the Jacks Point Zone (which includes Hanley's Farm and Willowpond), Northlake Zone, and Shotover Country Zone.

However short-term accommodation is a very site-specific matter, which makes it very difficult to provide generic advice. There are many, many other zones in the District Plan that will not be covered here. There are also 'visitor accommodation sub-zones' located within the zones we do cover here. Finally there are properties that already have resource consent that you may not be aware of, or specific restrictions that prevent short-term accommodation despite the rules (restrictions on the title for example).

Therefore we always offer free advice to inform you what the specific rules for your property are, and what you need to do to stay compliant with the new District Plan rules for short-term accommodation.

Warning: This Environment Court decision is the end of a process that started in 2017. Now the rules are settled, we are also now expecting the Queenstown Lakes District Council to commence a wide-reaching enforcement operation on illegal Airbnbs. Council is under significant pressure from the community to take action on the rental housing crisis. Please ensure you are 100% compliant with the District Plan rules to avoid enforcement action. If you have been contacted by Council's Monitoring and Enforcement Team, please contact us for specific advice.

Firstly: Are You Wanting To Undertake a Homestay Activity?

Planning to live at your property full-time at the same time fee-paying short-term guests are staying? Renting out spare rooms or the sleepout while you also live there? That is called a 'Homestay' and is a bit different. See information about Homestays further down this page.

However if you are intending to rent out the whole property, the following is a summary of the new rules for the Lower Density Suburban Residential Zone, Medium Density Residential Zone, High Density Residential Zone, Jacks Point Zone (which also includes the Hanley's Farm and Willowpond subdivisions), Northlake Zone, and Shotover Country Zone. You can find out which zone your property is located in using Council's mapping viewer, or just ask us.

Low and Medium Density Residential Zones

[New Rules: Environment Court Decision]

Up to 90 Nights Per Year

Up to 90 nights per year of short-term rental (where the whole property is rented) means a formal resource consent may no longer be required, provided all the relevant standards can be met, with Council approval obtained by a streamlined (and lower cost) registration process. QLDC will increase the property rates by approximately 25% once the registration application is approved.

There are a number of standards to be met to be eligible to unlock the 90 nights/year. These include: maximum guest numbers (depending on the size of the property); max one group at a time; no heavy vehicles; no guest use of outdoor living areas during night-time hours plus related signage installed on the property; and rubbish/recycling bins to be only left out on the street on collection day. The registration application also requires the details of the appointed local property manager to be supplied to Council, and neighbours served formal notice of the activity.

For 90 nights/year or less (approved via the streamlined registration application), there is also an on-going annual requirement to file records of all letting for the previous year to the Council, and neighbours re-served written notice of the activity.

More Than 90 Nights Per Year

More than 90 nights/year of short-term accommodation legally requires a resource consent to be granted by Council. These applications often require the written approval of neighbours. There is no longer a 'hard line in the sand' of a maximum 180 nights/year of short-term accommodation (as was the case with the old rules). It is now all up to us to prepare a comprehensive application and for Council assessment of what they consider appropriate.

Through the resource consent process, Council will consider a number of matters including: the location, nature and scale of the activity; vehicle access and parking; rubbish and recycling management; outdoor activities and outdoor lighting; privacy for neighbours; guest management and complaints procedures; record-keeping and monitoring requirements.

Unit title complex note: If your property is in a unit title complex, you need to firstly check that none of the other units in the complex are undertaking short-term accommodation. The '90 nights/year without consent' applies to the entire unit title complex and not each individual unit. So if another unit is being used for short-term accommodation, the 90-night allocation is 'extinguished' and a resource consent is required to use your unit for short-term accommodation, even for less than 90 nights/year.

More Than 90 Nights Per Year

More than 90 nights/year of short-term accommodation legally requires a resource consent to be granted by Council. Compared to the Low/Medium Density Residential Zones, it is easier to gain resource consent for more than 180 nights/year of short-term accommodation (often up to 365 nights/year).

Through the resource consent process, Council will consider a number of matters including: the location, nature and scale of the activity; vehicle access and parking; rubbish and recycling management; outdoor activities and outdoor lighting; privacy for neighbours; guest management and complaints procedures; record-keeping and monitoring requirements.

Unit title complex note: The same unit title complex rule applies as for the Low/Medium Density zones above.

Northlake Zone

[Northlake Rules]
  • No resource consent required for up to 90 nights per calendar year provided the house is either a free-standing or duplex dwelling (apartments and townhouses are not eligible and require resource consent).
  • To unlock the 90 nights/year, the property needs to be firstly registered as a holiday home with Council. QLDC will increase the property rates by approximately 25% once the registration application is approved.
  • Records of all letting must be kept; minimum one on-site car park available for guests.
  • Resource consent is legally required for more than 90 nights/year. This is a difficult consent to obtain (except in one central area of Northlake). Written approval of the neighbours is likely to be required.

Shotover Country Zone

[Shotover Country Rules]
  • No resource consent required for up to 90 nights per calendar year provided the house is either a free-standing or duplex dwelling (apartments and townhouses are not eligible and require resource consent).
  • To unlock the 90 nights/year, the property needs to be firstly registered as a holiday home with Council. QLDC will increase the property rates by approximately 25% once the registration application is approved.
  • Records of all letting must be kept; minimum one on-site car park available for guests.
  • Resource consent is legally required for more than 90 nights/year. This is generally a difficult consent to obtain in Shotover Country. Written approval of the neighbours is very likely to be required.

Homestay

A homestay is a form of short-term accommodation where you live on the property full-time, and rent out a spare room, sleepout, or flat on the same property for short-term accommodation. Think of it like a traditional bed and breakfast.

  • A homestay activity generally does not require a resource consent from Council, provided the maximum number of paying guests on the property at any one time does not exceed five (5).
  • There is no limit on the number of nights per year that a homestay activity can operate (no 90 nights/year or 180 nights/year cap). A homestay can operate year-round.
  • It is important that you are living on the property full-time in order to be eligible to be classified as a homestay. If no one is living on the property full-time, it cannot be classified as a homestay.
  • Before commencing a homestay activity, the property needs to be registered as a homestay with Council. Once approved, Council will increase your property rates by approximately 25%.
  • There is also not to be any heavy vehicle movements (by vehicles capable of carrying more than 12 people), and up-to-date records of the activity need to be kept and supplied to Council upon request.
  • If you would like to run a homestay activity with more than 5 guests, a resource consent is required from Council.

Do I Need Professional Holiday Home Management?

You will require someone who is locally based (full-time) to manage the property and quickly respond to any complaints. This could be yourself if you live locally, or an appointed local property manager. As part of the resource consent application (or registration application if less than 90 nights/year is sought), we need to supply details on how the property will be carefully managed when used for short-term accommodation, so as to not affect neighbours or the wider neighbourhood.

The majority of clients we work with choose to have their holiday home managed by a professional property management agency, and we incorporate this into the application. A property manager will assist you in meeting the requirements of your resource consent, in addition to promoting your property.
