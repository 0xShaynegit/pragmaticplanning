5 Costly Mistakes Property Buyers Make in Queenstown (and How to Avoid Them)

From skipping a LIM to assuming that view will last forever: the five most expensive mistakes we see property buyers encounter in the Queenstown Lakes District, and what you should do instead.

[TL;DR: 5 Mistakes]
  • 1. No LIM (takes 3-10 days, nominal cost)
  • 2. No due diligence (get a lawyer to review the LIM)
  • 3. Assumptions ("all must be fine if they're selling")
  • 4. Relying on a view (neighbours can build within permitted rules)
  • 5. Not anticipating change (Queenstown grows fast)

We regularly provide advice to soon-to-be property owners seeking their dream home in the Queenstown Lakes District. Common questions include what can I do on my property, can I subdivide, and what can my neighbour potentially do? These purchasers walk away with a better understanding of their target property and are therefore better informed to make the right decision.

Unfortunately throughout our years of professional practice we sometimes meet people who have purchased a property without advice and things have not gone so well. These troubles often come down to one of the following five mistakes.

Mistake 1: Not Obtaining a LIM

A LIM (Land Information Memorandum) is a document prepared by the Council in relation to a particular property. It provides a current snapshot of all the information the Council holds about that property. You can expect a LIM to contain information about infrastructure to and on the site (stormwater, etc), relevant resource and building consents, zoning and rating information, known natural hazards, and any other information the Council considers relevant.

Compared to the overall value of the property purchase, the nominal LIM fee is an absolute drop in the bucket.

Lesson: Make sure you obtain a LIM (which takes between 3 and 10 working days) prior to settling on a property.

Mistake 2: Lack of Due Diligence

The LIM you have just obtained will include a wealth of information. Unfortunately that information is often not in plain English and will refer to permits and consents issued under other Acts of Parliament. We strongly recommend engaging a lawyer to review the details of the LIM and property from a legal standpoint.

For example, there may be a resource consent in place on the property that consented the existing house, with on-going technical monitoring requirements. You need to know exactly what these requirements will be to understand the true cost of owning the property. An experienced lawyer will be able to undertake due diligence and cut through the technical language for you.

Lesson: You would not buy a secondhand car without a pre-purchase inspection. Make sure you do your due diligence before committing to what is likely the biggest purchase of your life.

Mistake 3: Making Assumptions

Assumptions are killer. Too often we have seen people purchase a property with the assumption that everything will be in order.

One expensive example involves a building in Hawea. The property was purchased with a single cottage in a charming white colour. Due to the assumption that "if the owner is selling, all must be fine with the Council," the owner was in for a shock when the Council monitored the property and asked why the house had not been repainted in a dull brown colour. It turned out that resource consent was granted to locate the cottage onto the site on the condition that it be painted brown and the roof replaced.

Obtain a LIM, undertake due diligence, understand all conditions of any consents on the property, and check that any other buildings or structures on the property already have consent or do not require consent.

Lesson: The seller is the seller. You as the buyer need to ensure you are not blinded by assumptions.

Mistake 4: Believing That Amazing View Will Be Around Forever

The majority of serious disputes between neighbours in the Queenstown Lakes District relate to building and development that restricts a once-amazing view. Views of our lakes and mountains are a serious privilege of living here, and there is nothing better than enjoying that privilege from your own living room. However, too often neighbours fall out when this view becomes restricted.

You need to understand that often these views are dependent on your neighbour not building something they are completely entitled to build within the permitted planning rules. The District Plan provides a defined set of building rights that your neighbour can exercise without your approval. Furthermore, they can apply for a resource consent to build beyond this box, and the Council does not necessarily have to consider that you are adversely affected.

Trees are another common view obstruction and are generally not controlled by the District Plan or any Council bylaws. Any disputes are a civil matter between the parties.

Lesson: Understand the planning and development rules before you purchase the property and accept that, unfortunately, an amazing view is often nothing more than a luxury you have no permanent right to.

Mistake 5: Not Anticipating the Future Environment Around You

Things change, and they always do. The once sleepy towns of the District are no longer and continue to grow at pace. Areas once used for grazing now contain multi-million dollar developments. People often buy property without truly understanding how much their surroundings can change and how quickly it can happen.

  • Stay informed about the District Plan review process and Council communications. This will directly affect your built surroundings into the future, and you can have a say in the process.
  • Keep in touch with your neighbours and discuss future plans. Alternatively, there is a way to find out what your neighbours are up to using QLDC's publicly accessible online tools.
  • Most importantly: expect things to change. Try to foresee what you consider the worst-case scenario and plan accordingly.

Lesson: Things change. Be ready for it.
