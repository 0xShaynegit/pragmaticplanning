Airbnb & Short-Term Accommodation Consents

Queenstown Lakes' short-term accommodation rules are among the most complex in the country. We specialise in helping property owners obtain resource consent for visitor accommodation, providing clear advice and managing the process from initial assessment through to approval.

[Quick Takeaways]
  • Most residential properties in QLDC require consent to list on Airbnb or similar platforms
  • Owner-occupied homes may qualify for homestay provisions without consent
  • Holiday homes let while the owner is absent almost always require a visitor accommodation consent
  • Breaches can result in Council enforcement action and retrospective consent costs

The QLDC Short-Term Accommodation Rules

Queenstown Lakes District Council introduced specific rules for short-term visitor accommodation in the Operative District Plan, and these rules have been progressively tightened as the district's housing crisis has deepened. The Proposed District Plan (PDP) carries similar provisions, and the rules apply across most residential zones in the district.

The core issue is activity status. In the Low Density Residential Zone, Medium Density Residential Zone, and most rural-residential zones, short-term visitor accommodation is a non-complying activity without a resource consent. This means Council can refuse consent and is not required to grant it simply because effects are minor.

The rules distinguish between different types of short-term letting based on whether the owner lives on site, the number of guests, the frequency of lettings, and whether the property is the owner's principal place of residence. Getting this assessment wrong at the start is the single most common and costly mistake property owners make.

Homestay vs Visitor Accommodation

Permitted

Homestay

A homestay is where the owner of the property lives on site and lets rooms to paying guests. Under the QLDC District Plan, a genuine homestay where the owner is present can be a permitted activity, meaning no resource consent is needed. The key tests are: the owner is residing at the property as their principal residence, the number of guests does not exceed the permitted threshold, and the activity does not generate effects beyond what is normal for a residential neighbourhood.

Airbnb listings that meet these criteria may not require consent, but getting confirmation is important before listing. We assess each situation on its merits.

Requires Consent

Visitor Accommodation (Unhosted)

Where the owner is not present, the entire property or a self-contained unit is let to visitors for short-term stays. This is the typical Airbnb or holiday home scenario. In residential zones across Queenstown Lakes, this is a discretionary or non-complying activity requiring resource consent.

The application must demonstrate that the activity will not create adverse effects on the residential character of the neighbourhood, that parking and access are adequate, and that the frequency and scale of letting is consistent with the zone's residential purpose.

Which Zones Are Affected

The rules apply differently across the district's zones. The most commonly affected properties are in:

  • Low Density Residential Zone (Queenstown, Wanaka, Hawea Flat, Albert Town): Unhosted visitor accommodation is non-complying. Consents are granted but the threshold is high.
  • Medium Density Residential Zone: Similar rules. Applications must demonstrate the activity fits the residential character of the area.
  • Rural Residential and Large Lot Residential Zones: Visitor accommodation may have a higher threshold for consent, particularly in areas close to urban centres.
  • Jacks Point, Hanley's Farm, Quail Rise: These areas have their own structure plans and rules. Some are more permissive for visitor accommodation; others are not. We have worked in all of them.
  • Wanaka Residential Zones: Similar rules to Queenstown but with different character considerations due to the different nature of the township.

What We Do

We have handled a large number of visitor accommodation resource consents across the district since 2015. Our approach is straightforward:

  1. We assess your specific property and proposed letting arrangement against the current District Plan rules.
  2. We advise on the realistic prospects of consent and the activity status that applies.
  3. If consent is viable, we prepare a complete application including the AEE, any necessary management plan for the property's use, and supporting information.
  4. We manage the process with QLDC through to a decision.

Where consent is not viable or the costs outweigh the benefit, we tell you that clearly at the outset. We do not take on applications we do not think can succeed.
