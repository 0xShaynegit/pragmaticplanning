[Subdivisions] Queenstown Lakes June 2026

The Subdivision Revolution: Unlocking Density in the Whakatipu Basin

In February 2026, QLDC formally adopted the Urban Intensification Variation. This was not a minor policy tweak. It was a fundamental re-zoning of the urban environment. If you hold land in Queenstown, Wanaka, or Hawea, your highest and best use calculation likely changed overnight.

[2026 Subdivision Update]
  • Height uplift: MDRZ limits have jumped from 8m to 11m (plus 1m for pitched roofs), enabling genuine three-storey development
  • Density shift: The "one unit per 250m2" rule has been largely removed in Medium Density zones, favouring site-specific design
  • The goal: Move from a single-dwelling title to a multi-unit asset in the shortest possible timeframe

The 11m Era: Vertical Opportunity in the MDRZ

For years, the 8-metre height limit was the glass ceiling of Queenstown development. It forced compromises, squeezed floor-to-ceiling heights, and made genuine three-storey apartment living nearly impossible without complex non-complying applications.

Under the 2026 Urban Intensification Variation, the Medium Density Residential Zone has seen a standard uplift to 11 metres. This extra 3 metres is the difference between a cramped duplex and a high-yield, three-storey multi-unit development. An additional 1 metre is often available for pitched roof designs, allowing for the alpine character QLDC still expects while maximising internal volume.

The important caveat: height is enabled, not guaranteed. The Planning Bill 2026 emphasises outcome-led design, and the Council looks closely at urban design frameworks. An 11-metre build that creates outlook space conflicts, recession plane breaches, or poor street interface can still be dragged into a notified hearing. Our role is to ensure your design meets the new height limit while clearing every other standard that could trigger notification.

Density Unleashed: Beyond the 250m2 Trap

The removal of the rigid density requirements in the MDRZ is the most significant change in the 2026 framework. Previously, the one-unit-per-250m2 net area rule locked in development yields and made infill in established areas mathematically difficult.

MDRZ and HDRA

Outcome-Based Density

The focus has shifted from "how many units fit the maths" to "how many units can the site's infrastructure and design support." In many zones, up to three residential units per site are now a permitted activity, provided they meet bulk and location standards. This makes backyard subdivisions in Fernhill, Frankton, and Wanaka South significantly more viable than they were 12 months ago.

Important Distinction

Building Density vs. Title Subdivision

The density rule is relaxed, but the minimum lot size of 250m2 for the MDRZ and 450m2 for the Suburban Residential Zone remain as baselines for fee-simple titles. You can build more units on a site, but if you want separate titles for each unit, minimum lot sizes still apply. Navigating the gap between building density and title subdivision is where most projects fail without expert planning input.

The Path to Section 224(c): Managing the Lifecycle

A subdivision is not finished when resource consent is granted. That is just the starting gun. The real work happens in the journey toward the Section 224(c) certificate, which is the document that unlocks new titles from LINZ.

  1. Resource consent (the permission): We secure your subdivision consent, setting out the conditions covering servicing, access, and development contributions.
  2. Section 223 (the plan): Your surveyor prepares the title plan. This must be lodged within five years of the consent being granted.
  3. Section 224(c) (the compliance): The QLDC subdivision inspector confirms that every condition has been met. Every pipe laid, every driveway sealed, every development contribution paid. Only then can LINZ issue new titles.

With the 2026 to 2027 Long Term Plan, QLDC has adjusted infrastructure levies. For a new residential unit in Queenstown, development contributions typically range from $25,000 to $45,000 per equivalent residential unit. We provide a contribution forecast at the outset so there are no six-figure surprises at the 224(c) stage.

Strategic Risk: The Section 37 Red Card

In the rush to capitalise on the 2026 height uplifts, many developers make a critical error: they start building before the subdivision titles are clear. This triggers a Section 37 certificate under the Building Act, which freezes your building consent until the resource consent issues are fully resolved.

We specialise in integrated applications, running your land use and subdivision consents in parallel to ensure the Section 37 is cleared before the first shovel hits the dirt. This parallel pathway saves months on complex multi-unit projects.

Exception

The Arrowtown Exception

The 2026 UIV does not apply blanket 11-metre rules to Arrowtown. The Arrowtown Suburban Residential Zone retains a tiered approach ranging from 6.5 metres to 8 metres, reflecting the historic character of the township. Applying Queenstown-style density in Arrowtown is a fast track to a declined decision. We know exactly where the UIV boundaries change.
