Open for Submissions: Queenstown Lakes Proposed District Plan Stage 3

QLDC formally notified Stage 3 of the Proposed District Plan on 19 September 2019. A summary of the key changes: Settlement Zone, Hawea and Albert Town rezoning, Wahi Tupuna, and Residential Design Guidelines.

[TL;DR: Stage 3 Key Changes]
  • Settlement Zone: replaces Township zones (Glenorchy, Kinloch, Kingston, Luggate, Makarora)
  • Albert Town + Hawea: rezoned to LDSR (1 dwelling per 300m2 possible)
  • Hawea/Albert Town: short-term accommodation requires consent under new zone
  • Wahi Tupuna overlay: more restrictive rules near lakes and rivers
  • Residential Design Guidelines: apply to multi-unit and non-complying proposals
  • Submissions closed 18 November 2019

Archived: Stage 3 was notified on 19 September 2019 with submissions closing on 18 November 2019. This article is retained for reference. For information on the current state of the District Plan, contact QLDC or speak to a planning consultant.

QLDC has formally publicly notified Stage 3 of its Proposed District Plan on 19 September 2019. This is the third stage of the District Plan review, with Stages 1 and 2 at a mixed stage of having been implemented/in effect, or appealed to the Environment Court.

Stage 3 is a real mixed bag of provisions. Here is a summary of the most significant changes.

New Settlement Zone

[Settlement Zone: Glenorchy, Kinloch, Kingston, Luggate, Makarora]

What Changes

The proposed new Settlement Zone largely replaces the existing Township zonings of Glenorchy, Kinloch, Kingston, Luggate, and Makarora. The provisions are largely similar to the current rules, but with the following key changes:

  • Easier to obtain resource consent for a residential flat (granny flat) at your property
  • Slight relaxation in boundary setback (2m)
  • Short-term accommodation for up to 90 nights per year is proposed as a permitted activity (no resource consent required, subject to compliance with standards)
  • The Glenorchy requirement for a gable roof form with a minimum pitch of 25 degrees still applies

Re-Zoning of Albert Town and Hawea

[Up-Zoning: Albert Town + Hawea]

Significant Development Uplift

QLDC is proposing to re-zone Hawea and Albert Town to the Lower Density Suburban Residential Zone (LDSR). This will bring these areas in line with the rest of Wanaka.

Under the current rules: Only one dwelling per 800m2 of land is permitted, meaning most properties are single-dwelling capable with no residential flats.

Under the proposed LDSR rules: Up to one dwelling per 300m2 (subject to resource consent), each with a residential flat. This represents a significant up-zoning of both Albert Town and Hawea.

The trade-off for short-term accommodation: Under the LDSR zone, a resource consent will be required to rent out your entire house for short-term accommodation. The 90-day permitted allowance that currently applies will no longer be available for Albert Town and Hawea properties.

New Wahi Tupuna Chapter

[New: Wahi Tupuna Overlay]

Kai Tahu Cultural Heritage Areas

QLDC is proposing to introduce a new Wahi Tupuna chapter. This chapter identifies specific wahi tupuna areas with an overlay on the planning maps, generally around the edges of lakes and rivers and up valleys. Urban areas are largely excluded, except for Glenorchy, Kingston, and a portion of Hawea.

Land within a wahi tupuna area will fall under a more restrictive resource management regime. The following are likely to be affected and may require consultation with Manawhenua and/or a cultural impact assessment:

  • Farm buildings will require resource consent
  • Increased setback of buildings from water bodies
  • Earthworks restricted to a maximum of 10m3 before resource consent is required
  • Subdivision of land in wahi tupuna areas

Residential Design Guidelines

[New: Residential Design Guidelines]

Quality Residential Neighbourhoods

QLDC is proposing to implement new Residential Design Guidelines applying to the High Density Residential, Medium Density Residential, and Lower Density Suburban Residential Zones. These guidelines will be built into the District Plan as part of the assessment for a proposal where:

  • Multiple residential units are proposed on a site
  • Commercial activities are proposed
  • Various non-compliances with development standards are proposed (setbacks from boundaries, maximum building coverage, building length, etc)

The guidelines aim to ensure the creation of high quality residential neighbourhoods.
